Termites (white ants)

Termites don't knock.

They live in the soil and come up unseen, through mud tubes, to the wood in your house. The damage often shows only when a door frame sounds hollow. Spraying the wood doesn’t reach the colony. The soil has to be treated.

Pre-construction treatment

Call us before the first concrete goes in.

Anti-termite treatment for a new building is done in stages as it goes up. Tell your contractor or engineer early.

Plan the treatment with us
  1. 01

    Foundation trenches

    When the trenches and column pits are dug, before the first concrete.

    The bottom and sides of every trench and pit are soaked with termiticide, so the foundation sits in treated soil.

  2. 02

    Backfill

    While the trenches are filled back around the foundation.

    The soil going back in around the foundation is treated as it is filled, leaving no untreated layer.

  3. 03

    Plinth fill

    After the plinth is filled with earth, before the floor concrete.

    The whole top surface of the plinth fill is treated. This is the soil directly under your floor.

  4. 04

    Wall and floor junction

    When the walls are up and the floor is being finished.

    The line where walls meet the floor gets its own treatment. Termites use this small gap to get inside.

  5. 05

    Around the outside

    At the end, when the plinth and the outside apron are done.

    The soil along the outside of the building is treated, closing the last way in from the garden.

Foundation trenches Backfill Plinth fill Wall and floor junction Around the outside

Stages as described in IS 6313 (Part 2), the Indian Standard for anti-termite soil treatment.

Is the inspection really free?

Yes. We come to your home, look at the problem and tell you what it needs and the price. There are no visiting charges, and you decide afterwards.

How do I know it is termites?

Look for thin mud tubes running up walls or behind skirting, door frames that sound hollow when tapped, wood that crumbles into powder, and small wings near lights after the first rains. Send us a photo on WhatsApp if you are not sure.

What is the difference between pre-construction and post-construction treatment?

Pre-construction treatment is for a building that is going up. The soil is treated in stages, from the foundation trenches to the plinth. Post-construction treatment is for a building that is already standing. The area is cleaned first, then small holes along the floor–wall line are filled with termiticide, and affected door and window frames are treated.

I am building a house. When should I call?

Before the first concrete goes into the foundation, ideally when the trenches are being dug. The treatment is done in stages as the building goes up, so tell your contractor or engineer early.

What does it cost?

The price depends on the area to be treated, whether the building is new or already built, and how far the termites have spread. You get the price at the free inspection, before any work starts.

How long does the work take?

It depends on the size of the building and how far the termites have spread. One customer wrote on Google that the crew "finished the job in about 5 hrs". Ask at the inspection how long your house will take.

Is it safe for children and pets?

For home pests we use odourless, government-approved chemicals. Ask the technician which rooms to keep children and pets out of, and for how long. They will explain it for the product used in your house.

Do you come on Sundays?

Our Google listing shows us open 24 hours, every day. Call and we will fix a time that suits you. One customer wrote that we came for the inspection on a Sunday.

Which areas do you serve?
